Chapter 1. Introduction

KAthenaeum turns Wikipedia, downloaded once as a Kiwix ZIM file, into a library on your own computer. You can read and search it without a connection, and ask it questions in plain language: a small language model, running locally through Ollama, writes the answer from the articles, and KAthenaeum links every sentence to the article it came from.

Nothing you read, search for or ask leaves your computer. There is no account and no cloud service. The only things KAthenaeum fetches from the Internet are those you ask for: the Kiwix catalog of libraries and the libraries you download, map regions, and a weekly check for newer versions of your libraries, which you can turn off.

What you can do with it:

  • Read any Wikipedia library, with its pictures, in your desktop's colours.

  • Search every library at once, by title or by the text of every article.

  • Ask questions and get answers with citations, from all your libraries or from one article.

  • Keep research projects: articles, saved passages, notes, chats, and documents written in Kate's editor, with AI drafts.

  • Browse offline maps, and click a city, a building or a lake to read about it.

  • Manage your libraries in the Library Manager: download, update, add and remove them.

KAthenaeum is the desktop version of Athenaeum, a web application for a home server. It is built for laptops: it needs no graphics card, and it never builds an index before you can use a library.
