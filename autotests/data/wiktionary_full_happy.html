English

Etymology

From Middle English happy, equivalent to hap (“luck”) + -y.

Pronunciation

  • (Received Pronunciation) IPA: /ˈhæpi/
  • (General American) IPA: /ˈhæpi/, [ˈhæpi]

Adjective

happy (comparative happier or more happy)

  1. Having a feeling arising from a consciousness of well-being or of enjoyment; contented, joyous.
    Synonyms: cheerful, content
    Music makes me feel happy.
    • 1813, Jane Austen, Pride and Prejudice
      “…I am happy…”
  2. (archaic) Experiencing the effect of favourable fortune; favored by hap; lucky.[1]
    1. A sub-sense that is not shown.
  3. Willing.
    I'd be happy to help.
  4. A fourth sense.
  5. A fifth sense, beyond the four shown.

Usage notes

  1. Not a sense: a list in another section.

Translations

  1. German: glücklich

Noun

happy (plural happies)

  1. (colloquial) A happy event, thing, person, etc.
